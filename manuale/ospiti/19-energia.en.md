This house makes its own energy. It isn't a technical detail for enthusiasts: it's the
reason why, while you're here, **almost everything you switch on comes from the roof and
not from the grid**.

## How autonomous it is

- **About 90% of the year** the house runs **off-grid**, that is, on its own energy.
- **In summer it reaches 100%**: from June to September the grid is effectively unused.

You don't have to do anything for this to happen — the system regulates itself. But if
you'd like to help, there's exactly one thing that matters, and it's just below.

## The only thing we ask

**Run the washing machine, the dryer and the dishwasher in the middle of the day**,
roughly **between 10am and 4pm**. In those hours the sun produces more than the house
consumes, and the surplus would otherwise go to the grid for very little.

In the evening the house lives off the battery: using it for a dryer cycle means reaching
dawn with less in reserve. No disaster, but if you can choose, choose daytime.

## How the system is built

- The **panels** on the roof, with **optimisers** on every module — so shade on one panel
  doesn't penalise the whole string.
- A **10 kW SolarEdge** inverter, which handles production.
- A **12 kW Deye** hybrid inverter, which manages the **battery** and the **backup**.
- A **battery of about 16 kWh**, storing the day's sun for the evening and the night.

## If the power goes out

The house **keeps running**: the Deye disconnects from the grid and feeds the loads from
the battery, usually without you noticing.

One thing to know, though: during a blackout **the panels stop producing**. It's a
consequence of how the system is wired — the SolarEdge needs the grid as a reference to
work. So in those hours the house lives **only on what's already in the battery**. If the
blackout lasts, go easy on the heavy loads.

## The meters you'll see around

Besides the main meter there are **two additional meters**, which separately measure the
two biggest consumptions in the house:

- one **next to the white Tesla**, for charging the car;
- one **in the laundry**, for the washing machine and dryer.

They only measure: **there's nothing to touch and nothing to reset**. If you notice them
and wonder what they are, now you know.
